# Variance-swap replication and the annualized 30-day horizon

**Tracker task:** W2-09

**Workstream:** VIX mathematics

**Role:** Econometrics

**Dependency:** W1-06

**Status:** Methodology-ready technical note; no change to the locked empirical protocol

## Methodological conclusion

VIX is constructed from a broad strip of SPX option prices because, under
standard no-arbitrage conditions, options across strikes statically replicate
a log payoff. Combined with dynamic trading in the underlying, that log payoff
is linked to realized quadratic variation. The resulting option-strip value is
therefore interpretable as a risk-neutral, variance-like quantity without
inverting a Black--Scholes formula for any single option.

Cboe computes variance at two option expirations that bracket 30 calendar days,
interpolates **total variance** to 30 days, and then divides by \(30/365\) to
quote an annualized variance rate. Consequently,

\[
IVAR_t=(VIX_t/100)^2
\]

is an annualized decimal variance, while its 30-day total-variance counterpart
is \((30/365)IVAR_t\). The empirical target uses the same conversion in the
opposite direction:

\[
RVAR^{CC}_{t,30c}
=\frac{365}{30}
\sum_{d:t<d\leq t+30c}r_d^2.
\]

The factor \(365/30\) changes units; it does not turn a 30-day forecast into a
one-year forecast and does not add observations beyond the 30-day interval.

## 1. Variance-swap payoff and fair strike

A variance swap exchanges a realized-variance leg for a fixed variance strike.
Writing \(\tau=T-t\) in years, an idealized annualized realized-variance payoff
is

\[
V_{t,T}=\frac{1}{\tau}[\log S]_{t,T},
\]

where \([\log S]_{t,T}\) is the quadratic variation of log price over the
contract horizon. A swap with variance notional \(N_{\mathrm{var}}\) pays

\[
N_{\mathrm{var}}\bigl(V_{t,T}-K_{\mathrm{var},t,T}\bigr)
\]

to the long-variance side at \(T\). For a zero-value swap at inception, its
fair strike is the appropriately discounted risk-neutral expectation of the
realized leg. With deterministic rates this simplifies to

\[
K_{\mathrm{var},t,T}=E_t^Q[V_{t,T}].
\]

With stochastic rates, the corresponding expression is a \(T\)-forward,
discount-weighted expectation rather than an unqualified \(Q\)-expectation.
In neither case is the fair strike generally the physical expectation
\(E_t^P[V_{t,T}]\).

## 2. Why a strip of options reveals a variance-like price

### 2.1 Dynamic identity

For a strictly positive continuous price process, Itô's formula gives the
pathwise identity

\[
[\log S]_{t,T}
=2\int_t^T\frac{dS_u}{S_u}
-2\log\!\left(\frac{S_T}{S_t}\right).
\]

The first term can be generated by a dynamically rebalanced position in the
underlying. The terminal nonlinear term is a log contract. After carrying and
dividend terms are represented by the date-\(T\) forward
\(F_{t,T}\), the expected identity can be written schematically as

\[
E_t^Q\!\left[[\log S]_{t,T}\right]
=-2E_t^Q\!\left[
\log\!\left(\frac{S_T}{F_{t,T}}\right)
\right].
\]

This step is the bridge from future quadratic variation to a European terminal
payoff. It is not a Black--Scholes assumption about constant volatility or
lognormal returns.

### 2.2 Static replication of the log contract

For any positive terminal price \(x\), expansion around the forward \(F\)
gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
-\log(x/F)
={}&-\frac{x-F}{F}\\
&+\int_0^F\frac{(K-x)^+}{K^2}\,dK
+\int_F^\infty\frac{(x-K)^+}{K^2}\,dK.
\end{aligned}
\]

The first term is a forward payoff. The first integral is a continuum of puts
with strikes below \(F\), and the second is a continuum of calls with strikes
above \(F\). The curvature of the log payoff produces the \(1/K^2\) option
weights. Taking risk-neutral values, the forward term has zero value at its
delivery price. Under deterministic rate \(r\), the idealized annualized fair
variance strike is therefore

\[
K_{\mathrm{var},t,T}
=\frac{2e^{r\tau}}{\tau}
\left[
\int_0^F\frac{P_t(K,T)}{K^2}\,dK
+\int_F^\infty\frac{C_t(K,T)}{K^2}\,dK
\right].
\]

This formula supplies the central replication intuition:

1. future variance is linked to a log payoff plus dynamic underlying trading;
2. the log payoff is synthesized from out-of-the-money puts and calls across
   strikes; and
3. no-arbitrage option prices therefore encode a risk-neutral variance-like
   value.

“Model-free” refers to avoiding a particular parametric option-pricing model
for this extraction. It does not remove assumptions about no arbitrage,
European exercise, integrability, discounting, dividends, continuous strike
coverage, path behavior, or trading.

## 3. From the ideal strip to the operational VIX

For one option expiration with year fraction \(T_j\), Cboe's discrete-strike
variance calculation has the form

\[
\sigma_j^2
=\frac{2}{T_j}\sum_i
\frac{\Delta K_i}{K_i^2}e^{R_jT_j}Q_j(K_i)
-\frac{1}{T_j}
\left(\frac{F_j}{K_{0,j}}-1\right)^2.
\]

Here \(Q_j(K_i)\) is the methodology quote at strike \(K_i\),
\(\Delta K_i\) is its strike interval, \(F_j\) is the option-implied forward,
and \(K_{0,j}\) is the first listed strike at or below that forward. This is a
discrete, filtered implementation of the option-strip logic, not the implied
volatility of one option.

Let \(N_1<N_{30}<N_2\) be minutes to the two selected expirations and to the
30-day target, with

\[
N_{30}=43{,}200,
\qquad
N_{365}=525{,}600,
\qquad
T_j=N_j/N_{365}.
\]

Define interpolation weights

\[
w_1=\frac{N_2-N_{30}}{N_2-N_1},
\qquad
w_2=\frac{N_{30}-N_1}{N_2-N_1},
\qquad
w_1+w_2=1.
\]

Cboe interpolates the maturity-scaled quantities
\(T_j\sigma_j^2\), which are total variances, rather than interpolating
volatilities:

\[
A_{30}=w_1T_1\sigma_1^2+w_2T_2\sigma_2^2.
\]

It then annualizes that 30-day amount:

\[
\sigma_{30,\mathrm{ann}}^2
=\frac{N_{365}}{N_{30}}A_{30}
=\frac{365}{30}A_{30},
\]

and reports

\[
VIX=100\sqrt{\sigma_{30,\mathrm{ann}}^2}.
\]

Thus the exact unit mapping used in this project is

\[
IVOL_t=\frac{VIX_t}{100},
\qquad
IVAR_t=IVOL_t^2=\sigma_{30,\mathrm{ann}}^2,
\qquad
A^{IV}_{t,30c}=\frac{30}{365}IVAR_t.
\]

VIX is often described as expected volatility, but mathematically it is
100 times the square root of an annualized option-implied variance quantity.
It is not generally \(100E_t^Q[\sqrt{V_{t,T}}]\).

## 4. Matching the empirical 30-calendar-day target

For each valid end-of-day origin \(t\), the project includes close-to-close
log returns whose return-ending exchange dates satisfy

\[
t<d\leq t+30\text{ calendar days}.
\]

The unannualized horizon amount and annualized rate are

\[
HVAR^{CC}_{t,30c}
=\sum_{d:t<d\leq t+30c}r_d^2,
\qquad
RVAR^{CC}_{t,30c}
=\frac{HVAR^{CC}_{t,30c}}{30/365}.
\]

This construction has four deliberate implications.

1. **Calendar maturity is fixed.** The number of exchange-session returns can
   vary with weekends and holidays. That variation does not change the
   economic maturity being matched.
2. **The annualizer is fixed.** The primary target always uses \(365/30\), not
   \(252/n_t\), where \(n_t\) is the realized number of sessions. A
   session-dependent denominator would redefine the target as a trading-time
   rate.
3. **There is no same-date leakage.** Return \(r_t=\log(C_t/C_{t-1})\) already
   contains information through the origin close, so it is excluded. The
   first eligible return ends strictly after \(t\).
4. **Annualization is only rescaling.** Comparing annualized quantities is
   algebraically equivalent to comparing their 30-day amounts:

\[
IVAR_t-RVAR^{CC}_{t,30c}
=\frac{365}{30}
\left(A^{IV}_{t,30c}-HVAR^{CC}_{t,30c}\right).
\]

For example, \(VIX=20\) maps to \(IVOL=0.20\),
\(IVAR=0.04\), and 30-day implied total variance
\(0.04(30/365)\approx0.003288\). Squaring the displayed index level directly
would introduce a factor-of-10,000 unit error.

Daily close-to-close returns also preserve variation accumulated between
closes: a return ending after a weekend incorporates the movement since the
previous exchange close. No synthetic weekend zero returns are inserted.

## 5. Why 21 trading days is robustness rather than the primary horizon

The conventional one-trading-month target is

\[
RVAR^{CC}_{t,21t}
=\frac{252}{21}\sum_{j=1}^{21}r_{t+j}^2.
\]

Its year fraction, \(21/252=1/12\), is numerically close to but not identical
to \(30/365\). More importantly, it fixes the number of exchange sessions,
whereas VIX fixes calendar maturity. The 21-session design is therefore a
useful test of sensitivity to the common trading-month approximation, but it
does not replace the primary exact-30-calendar-day match. The two annualizers
must not be mixed.

## 6. Interpretation and replication basis

The idealized variance-swap equation and the operational VIX are related, not
identical. Exact replication uses a continuum of strikes and a precisely
defined variance payoff. VIX uses listed strikes, quote filters, forward
construction, two expirations, and a specific constant-maturity interpolation.
The empirical realized leg uses daily close-to-close squared returns rather
than the exact monitoring and settlement conventions of a traded variance
contract.

Material approximation channels include:

- discrete and bounded strikes, especially sparse deep-tail quotes;
- bid--ask spreads, quote timing, filtering, and interpolation;
- stochastic rates, dividends, and forward-price estimation;
- jumps and the jump correction to the continuous-path log-contract identity;
- discrete monitoring and the difference between quadratic variation and
  close-to-close squared returns; and
- the date-level historical VIX close relative to the S&P 500 cash close.

Accordingly, this project does not call \(IVAR_t\) the exact replication cost
of \(RVAR^{CC}_{t,30c}\). It uses

\[
VRP_t^X=IVAR_t-RVAR^{CC}_{t,30c}
\]

as the locked **ex-post variance-risk-premium proxy**. The theoretical premium
remains

\[
E_t^Q[V_{t,T}]-E_t^P[V_{t,T}].
\]

The observed proxy additionally contains physical forecast error and
contract, measurement, and replication basis. A positive mean proxy is
consistent with compensation for variance and tail risk; it is not, by itself,
an exact variance-swap premium, an unbiased-forecast test, or evidence of
irrationality.

## 7. Methodology text

> VIX is obtained from a broad strip of SPX options rather than from the
> Black--Scholes implied volatility of one option. Under idealized
> no-arbitrage conditions, out-of-the-money puts and calls across strikes
> statically replicate a log payoff, which dynamic trading links to realized
> quadratic variation. Cboe implements a discrete version of this
> variance-replication logic at two expirations bracketing 30 calendar days,
> linearly interpolates maturity-scaled variance \(T\sigma^2\), and annualizes
> the resulting 30-day total variance. We therefore define
> \(IVAR_t=(VIX_t/100)^2\) and compare it with
> \(RVAR^{CC}_{t,30c}=(365/30)\sum_{t<d\leq t+30c}r_d^2\). Returns are selected
> by return-ending exchange date, so no return dated \(t\) enters the forward
> target. The annualization factor is a unit conversion from a 30-day amount
> to a yearly rate, not an extension of the forecast horizon. The number of
> sessions may vary because the primary estimand fixes calendar maturity; the
> next 21 trading days, annualized by \(252/21\), are retained as mandatory
> robustness. Because the operational index and close-to-close target are not
> literally the same traded contract, \(IVAR_t-RVAR^{CC}_{t,30c}\) is
> interpreted as an ex-post variance-risk-premium proxy rather than a direct
> observation of the conditional \(Q-P\) variance premium.
